Beginner Projects

Essential Woodworking Tools for Beginners

Seven tools cover almost every project a beginner builds in the first year. Buying beyond that list before your first project is the most common way new woodworkers overspend before they know which style of building they actually enjoy.

The seven essential tools

  1. Tape measure — for every cut on every project.
  2. Combination square — for marking a right angle and checking a cut afterward.
  3. Hand saw or circular saw — pick one to start; add the other once you know which cuts you make most.
  4. Drill/driver — for pilot holes and driving screws.
  5. Sandpaper or a random orbital sander — hand sanding works, but a sander saves real time past your first few projects.
  6. Bar or spring clamps — for holding glue-ups square while they cure.
  7. Wood glue — for every joint that isn't held by screws alone.

The one power tool worth buying first

Between a circular saw and a drill, the circular saw does more of the work a beginner needs — breaking sheet goods and boards down to size — while a basic drill/driver is inexpensive enough to buy alongside it. If your budget forces a choice between a circular saw and a table saw for straight cuts, see Table Saw vs. Circular Saw for a Beginner for the trade-offs.

Safety gear that isn't optional

  • Safety glasses, every time a blade or bit is spinning
  • Hearing protection, for any power tool used for more than a few minutes
  • A dust mask, for sanding and cutting manufactured sheet goods

Treat this gear as part of the tool list, not an add-on you'll get to later.

Tools to skip at the start

  • Table saw — expensive, and a circular saw with a straightedge guide covers most beginner cuts.
  • Planer and jointer — needed for milling rough lumber, which most beginners aren't buying yet.
  • Router — useful for edges and joinery, but it adds a learning curve better tackled after your first few projects.

Where quality matters, and where it doesn't

Spend on accuracy where a small error compounds: your tape measure and square. Spend less on clamps and sandpaper — buy a few cheap clamps now and add more as projects demand it, rather than buying a full set upfront.
