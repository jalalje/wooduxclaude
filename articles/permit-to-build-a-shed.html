Shed planning

Do You Need a Permit to Build a Shed?

Usually, the answer depends on size, setbacks, zoning, and whether you are adding electrical or plumbing.

Many people expect a quick yes-or-no. In real life, the rules vary by city and county. The safe move is to check the permit threshold before finalizing your shed plan.

Start with size

In many areas, sheds below a certain square footage are exempt from a full permit. Common thresholds fall around 100 to 200 square feet, but the exact number depends on the county, city code, and whether the structure is freestanding or attached.

Setbacks matter even when the permit is waived

  • Most places still require a minimum distance from property lines.
  • Some also require setbacks from utility easements, neighbor fences, and other structures.
  • Even a small shed can become noncompliant if placed in the wrong spot.

Electrical or plumbing can trigger a permit

If the shed will have power, lights, a mini-split, a sink, or plumbing, the rules often change even when the structure itself is small. In many jurisdictions, electrical work must be permitted and inspected separately from the building itself.

HOA and subdivision rules often add more restrictions

Even if the city says no permit is needed, your neighborhood association can still restrict size, location, appearance, or materials. It is common for a shed to be legal on paper but still not allowed in practice.

The smartest move before you build

Call the local building office or zoning office before you settle the design. A five-minute phone call can prevent a very expensive redesign.

Ask specifically about the shed size exemption, setbacks, and whether electrical use changes the requirement. That one answer can save you from building in the wrong location or with the wrong foundation.
