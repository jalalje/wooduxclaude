Table Saw vs. Circular Saw: What a Beginner Actually Needs First

A table saw is the default answer whenever someone asks what saw to buy first, and it is usually the wrong one. It is expensive, it takes up permanent floor space most garages cannot spare, and a track-guided circular saw handles the same cuts a beginner actually makes, for a fraction of the price and footprint.

What a track-guided circular saw covers

With a straightedge or track guide, a circular saw makes rip cuts and crosscuts on sheet goods and dimensional lumber that are just as accurate as a table saw for a beginner's purposes. It also breaks down full plywood sheets safely by yourself, which a table saw actually makes harder, not easier, without a large outfeed table.

Where a table saw genuinely wins

A table saw earns its space once you are cutting a high volume of repeated, identical rip cuts — cabinet-grade work, batches of the same part, or narrow strips that are awkward to guide a handheld saw through safely. It also makes certain joinery cuts (dados, rabbets with a stack cutter) more reliably repeatable than a router alone.

The real cost comparison

A decent circular saw and track together typically run $150–$250. An entry-level table saw that isn't a disappointment starts closer to $400–$600, before adding an outfeed table and dust collection it actually needs to be useful in a small shop. That gap is real money that can go toward lumber for your first several projects instead.

A simple decision rule

Start with a track-guided circular saw. Add a table saw later, specifically when a project's cut list makes it obvious you need one — repeated identical rip cuts, or joinery a router setup can't handle cleanly. Buying it in that order means neither tool sits unused.
