Best Beginner Outdoor Furniture Projects (And What They Cost in Lumber)

Outdoor furniture is a good place to start woodworking, for a reason people rarely say out loud: it doesn't need to be perfect. A slightly imperfect seam on a picture frame stands out at three feet. The same seam on a garden bench, viewed from six feet away in dappled light, does not. That forgiveness makes outdoor furniture a lower-pressure way to build real skill.

Planter box — easiest

Straight cuts, butt joints, and screws. A cedar planter box in a standard size runs roughly $25–$40 in lumber, depending on cedar prices in your area. This is the project to learn "measure twice, cut once" on before anything with real joinery.

Adirondack chair — moderate

More cuts and angles than a planter box, but still no advanced joinery — mostly straight cuts at fixed angles, assembled with exterior screws. Expect $60–$100 in cedar or pressure-treated lumber per chair. The main beginner mistake is skipping a template for the curved back slats and free-handing the angle, which is where most wasted boards come from.

Picnic table — moderate to involved

A picnic table introduces load-bearing joinery for the first time — the legs need to hold real weight under uneven pressure, not just static weight. Budget $100–$180 in lumber for a standard 6-foot table, and expect the leg assembly to take longer than the tabletop itself.

Pergola or arbor — most involved

This is where outdoor furniture crosses into small structure territory — post footings, level checks across a wider span, and larger dimensional lumber. Costs vary widely by size, from around $200 for a small arbor to $800+ for a full pergola, so this is the project to attempt after the three above, not before.

The wood choice matters more outdoors

Indoors, wood choice is mostly about looks. Outdoors, it determines how long the project lasts. Cedar and pressure-treated pine are the two realistic budget choices for ground contact and weather exposure — untreated pine or plywood will visibly degrade within a season or two outside.
