Which Power Tools to Buy for a Woodworking Shop, and in What Order

Buy tools in the order your projects need them, not in the order a "complete shop" list suggests. A dust collector bought before a table saw sits unused; a table saw bought before you've outgrown a circular saw is money spent on accuracy you don't need yet.

The five jobs every tool list covers

Every woodworking tool exists to do one of five jobs: cutting stock to size, joining pieces together, shaping edges and profiles, smoothing surfaces, or controlling dust. A shop doesn't need every tool in every category to start — it needs one working tool per job it actually does right now.

A buying order that unlocks the most projects per dollar

  1. A saw for breaking down stock — a circular saw first; see Table Saw vs. Circular Saw for a Beginner for when to move up.
  2. A drill/driver — for pilot holes and assembly, on nearly every project.
  3. Clamps — for glue-ups, before your first multi-piece build.
  4. A sander — once hand-sanding starts slowing you down more than it's worth.
  5. A router — for edges and joinery, once your projects call for a profile or a dado.
  6. A band saw — for curves and resawing, once a project needs a cut a straight blade can't make.
  7. A planer — once you're buying rough lumber instead of pre-surfaced boards.
  8. A dust collector — once you're running stationary machines often enough that a shop vac can't keep up.

Signs it's time to upgrade from a starter tool

Upgrade when the starter tool is limiting which projects you can take on, not on a schedule. A circular saw that can't hold a straight enough line for cabinet-grade joinery is a sign to look at a table saw or a track saw — see Track Saws for Woodworking: What to Look For. A hand-sanded finish that's taking longer than the build itself is a sign to add a sander.

What to skip until a project actually needs it

A drum sander, a jointer, and a dust collection system are all worth owning eventually, and none of them are worth buying before your first few projects show you need them. Spend that budget on lumber and clamps instead, early on.
